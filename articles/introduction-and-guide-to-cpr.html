Introduction and Guide to CPR

Over half a million cardiac arrests occur every year in the United States alone, so it is important to know what to do when you witness one. CPR can increase survival rates by up to three times, and it’s not just for first responders to do. Bystanders can take action once they learn the essentials of CPR.

Title and Overview

CPR stands for cardiopulmonary resuscitation. It is used when the heart stops beating completely or is not beating enough to deliver blood to the brain and other organs. CPR's main role is to keep blood flowing to the brain and vital organs, lowering the risk of brain damage until emergency responders arrive.

Before Starting

Before performing CPR, check the area to make sure you are safe. Do not put yourself at risk. Check for normal breathing. If the person is not breathing or is only gasping, start CPR immediately. Also call 9-1-1, or ask another person to do so and get an AED.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Check the scene to ensure it is safe to perform CPR.
  2. If the person appears unresponsive, check breathing and responsiveness by tapping their shoulders and shouting.
  3. If there is no response, call 9-1-1 immediately and begin CPR actions.
  4. Direct someone specific to get an AED and first aid kit if available.
  5. Kneel beside the person and place them on a firm, flat surface. Soft surfaces like beds or sofas reduce CPR effectiveness.

The following compression guidelines are from the American Red Cross:

  • Rate: 100 to 120 chest compressions per minute, 30 at a time.
  • Hands: Two hands centered on the chest.
  • Body: Lock your elbows and keep your shoulders directly over your hands.
  • Compression depth: At least 2 inches.
  • Allow full chest recoil after each compression.

To give 2 rescue breaths:

  1. Tilt the head back slightly to open the airway.
  2. Pinch the nose shut, seal your mouth over theirs, and give each breath over about 1 second.
  3. Check for chest rise and allow air to exit before repeating.
  4. If the first breath does not raise the chest, readjust head position and seal. If the second still does not raise the chest, the airway may be blocked.

Continue cycles of 30 compressions and 2 breaths. Use an AED as soon as one arrives. Minimize interruptions to compressions to under 10 seconds, turn on the AED, and follow its prompts.

Tips and Warnings

Most importantly, minimize interruptions during CPR. Every second without compressions means less oxygen reaching the brain and greater risk of brain damage before help arrives.

If you are untrained, you do not have to provide rescue breaths. Hands-only CPR is still effective and recommended. To keep the correct pace, many people use the beat of songs like "Stayin' Alive."

Push hard and fast. Compressions should be deep and consistent. Keep your elbows straight and use body weight rather than arm strength alone.

When assigning tasks, be direct. Do not shout "someone call 9-1-1" to a crowd. Point to a specific person and tell them to call immediately.

Summary Checklist

  • Make sure the scene is safe.
  • Check responsiveness and breathing.
  • Call 9-1-1 and get an AED.
  • Start compressions at 100 to 120 BPM, at least 2 inches deep.
  • Use 30 compressions to 2 breaths (breaths optional if you are not comfortable).
  • Use an AED and continue compressions until emergency help arrives.
