The Most Common Emergencies at Home

Household emergencies happen far too often, and many times, the people affected by them weren’t properly prepared. This can lead to emergencies like heart attacks and strokes, which are addressed in this article along with the medical procedures to prevent and save someone from these emergencies.

Household emergencies are incredibly common, and unfortunately many people are not prepared when they happen. The National Center for Health Statistics reports that in the United States there are over 155 million household medical emergencies, with 73% of life-threatening incidents occurring in private residences. Common emergencies at home include heart attacks, choking, strokes, allergic reactions, severe burns, and falls. While this list can feel overwhelming, even basic preparation can make a major difference.

Heart Attacks at Home

Heart attacks are especially common among people with existing heart conditions and can occur at any moment. Knowing warning signs and acting quickly can save lives. UMass Memorial Health advises calling 911 immediately if you suspect a heart attack and never letting the patient drive themselves to the hospital, as this creates serious risk for everyone involved.

While waiting for emergency responders, unlock the front door, keep a phone nearby, and be mindful of allergies if aspirin is considered.

Stroke Response and FAST

Stroke is another emergency that requires immediate action. If someone may be having a stroke, record the time symptoms began. According to the Mayo Clinic, common stroke symptoms include trouble speaking or understanding, numbness or paralysis, vision problems, severe headache, and trouble walking.

Use the FAST method:

  • Face: Ask the person to smile. Is one side of the face drooping?
  • Arms: Ask the person to raise both arms. Does one arm drift down?
  • Speech: Ask the person to repeat a simple phrase. Is speech slurred or unusual?
  • Time: If any of these signs appear, call 911 immediately.

Do not wait to see if symptoms pass on their own. As stroke goes untreated, the risk of brain damage and long-term disability rises sharply. Monitor the person closely until emergency help arrives.

Choking During Meals

Choking is dangerous because it happens suddenly. If someone is choking, stay calm so you can make better decisions. The Cleveland Clinic advises against first aid if the person is coughing with sound, and against leaving them alone, since they may clear the object on their own.

Do not blindly sweep inside the mouth because this can push the object deeper. Only remove the object if it is clearly visible.

If there are signs of complete choking, call 911 immediately and begin first aid with the phone on speaker:

  1. Stand slightly to the side and behind the person.
  2. Lean them slightly forward.
  3. Support the chest with your non-dominant hand.
  4. Give five back blows between the shoulder blades with the base of your dominant hand.
  5. If the object remains and you are trained, give five abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver).

Back blows and abdominal thrusts can be lifesaving but may also cause injury to organs or bones. Even if the person seems fine afterward, follow up with medical care.

Preparation Saves Lives

Household emergencies should never be taken lightly. One of the most important first steps is always calling 911. These incidents should also be followed up by a healthcare professional to protect long-term safety. Emergencies can happen to anyone at any time, and preparation helps people respond with clarity and care.
