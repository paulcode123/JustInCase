The Most Common Emergencies at Home

Household emergencies happen far too often, and many times, the people

Heart attacks are especially prevalent in those with heart conditions such as elderly folk, and can happen at any given moment. Knowing the warning signs and preparing properly can save a life. UMass Memorial Health instructs to call 911 immediately if you suspect someone is having a heart attack, and not allow the patient to drive to the hospital themselves as this can pose a great risk to them and those around them. While waiting for emergency help to arrive, ensure the front door is unlocked, a phone is with you, and keep allergens in mind as aspirin may be administered. A stroke is another common medical emergency that requires immediate action. If someone around you could be having a stroke, record the time symptoms start. According to the Mayo Clinic, common stroke symptoms include trouble speaking/understanding others, numbness or paralysis, eyesight problems, headaches, and difficulty walking. Remember the acronym “FAST” and administer the following: 1. Face: Ask the person to smile. Has their face or mouth dropped? 2. Arms: Ask the person to raise their arms. Can they do it? Does one arm droop? 3. Speech: Ask the person to repeat a simple phrase. Is their speech slurred? 4. Time: If you see any of these signs, immediately call 911. It is vital to call 911 immediately, and not wait to see if symptoms stop naturally. As time goes on and the stroke continues to go untreated, the risk of brain damage and disability increases dramatically. It is important to keep the patient under careful watch as emergency help is on the way. Choking is dangerous due to its sudden nature during meals. If someone around you is choking, remember to stay calm in order to make thoughtful decisions. The Cleveland Clinic writes to not attempt first aid if the individual is coughing with sound, and to not leave them alone during the incident. They are likely to cough up the object on their own.
