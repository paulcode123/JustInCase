Technology's Role in Modern Emergency Response

As humanity advances, so does technology. Learn about the different innovations that experts use to ensure our safety in this dangerous world.

Why Every Second Counts

What dictates between life and death may just be a matter of a few seconds, so it is crucial to waste as little time as possible during emergency situations. Luckily, in the modern world, there are numerous technologies that help emergency workers locate victims and assess their conditions. Advances in communications have enhanced efficiency and helped improve coordinated responses compared to decades ago.

First Responder Communications

One of the most crucial tech used by people today are digital radio systems. They provide a pathway for first responders to clearly communicate with one another in real time. The extensive use of cellular networks, with some using satellite communications, have helped increase reliability and speed.

Mapping and Situational Awareness

Another prominently used innovation are Geographic Information Systems, or GIS. They are utilized for location-related needs such as mapping disaster areas, predicting natural disasters, and search-and-rescue missions. Remote sensing technology is also very powerful, providing up-to-date images in areas where disaster struck, allowing for assessment of damage and formulating a plan.

Public Alert Systems

In order for rapid response, there must be ways to communicate with citizens. Different local governments may utilize different approaches to announce emergency updates. One common method is the usage of Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEAs), in which officials are able to send alerts to WEA-enabled devices quickly in certain locations. This method is not limited to local governments, but also the national government. For example, when the nation encounters a national emergency, the President or other alerting authorities will use WEAs to address the nation.

Another way to alert the people is through the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) Weather Radio All Hazards (NWR), a nationwide network that broadcasts official warnings or forecasts. In spite of its name, the NWR also issues non-weather emergencies such as public safety threats and other hazards.

Apps and Social Media in Emergencies

Additionally, mobile apps have also become increasingly popular for both emergency responders and the general public. Numerous features like warnings, navigation tools, and identification of disasters are implemented in these softwares. This allows for utilization of resources on a device that most people possess: a cellphone.

In recent years, social media has come to play a huge role in everyday life. Though it might not be a good resource to use for immediate emergencies, it can be used to spread information such as things to do during a disaster, pinpoint specific locations that need special attention, and inform citizens of recovery plans.
