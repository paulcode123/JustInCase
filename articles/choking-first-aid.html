Choking First Aid

Over 5,000 people in the United States die from choking every year. Many are aware of the risks, but few actually know what to do in case of emergency. Read this manual so you can be prepared to quickly take action and save somebody from choking – a couple seconds can be the difference between life and death.

Fiona Cheng Read Time: 2 minutes You walk in a restaurant and then your server sits you down. You pick up the menu and think about what your tastebuds are craving. Just as you signal a waiter over, you notice a frantic commotion growing in the far distance. You look over and see a man clutching both hands to his throat, his skin losing hue, turning grey, producing nothing but a strained, wheezing breath of air.

Then it hits you – you remember this all too well from your CPR certification course. Both hands gripping the throat. Unable to speak. Noisy, labored breathing. Lips turning blue. This is choking: every second counts, so don’t hesitate and save him.

If the person becomes unconscious

If you cannot get your arms all the way around their midsection

Rather than abdominal thrusts, start by placing the thrusts on their sternum (breastbone), just above where the lowest ribs meet, and apply firm thrusts. If the choking victim is an infant younger than 1 year old: Sit down and hold the infant face-down on your forearm. Using the base of your palm, gently but firmly thump 5 times on the middle of the back. If there is still no response, turn the infant face-up on your forearm with the head kept lower than the trunk. Place two fingers at the center of the breastbone, and deliver 5 quick chest compressions. Repeat the back blows and chest compressions until breathing resumes or emergency help arrives.

At last, the man coughs and reclaims their breathing. You have just saved someone’s life because you knew exactly what to do. Choking is one of the leading causes of accidental death, but it is also one of the most preventable. Informing yourself with articles on what to do is a strong first step, but the most optimal way is to get yourself certified. Look into a

CPR and first-aid course in your local area through the American Red Cross or American Heart Association. Don’t wait to learn until it's too late.
