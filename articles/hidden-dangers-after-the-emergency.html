Hidden Dangers After the Emergency Is "Over"

It is important to learn the potential risks and dangers that linger after an emergency. This article discusses the hidden dangers after emergencies, such as fires and strokes, have ended.

The flames have stopped, and the firefighters are cleaning up what remains of the place that was burnt down. One may assume that the scene is now safe after the fire stops. However, there are often hidden dangers and risks after an emergency that one may be exposed to if not aware. Not only can this be applied to fires, but after other types of emergencies as well such as a stroke or floods. It is important to stay aware of potential threats even after an emergency is over.

After a Fire: Toxic Exposure

After a fire, firefighters often face hidden risks after they leave a scene. According to the Colorado Firefighter Heart, Cancer, and Behavioral Health Benefits Trust, firefighters can potentially be exposed to toxic contaminants on their gear and equipment. These toxins can follow firefighters everywhere, including their trucks and their homes, as well as negatively affect bystanders and victims of the scene. Additionally, firefighters and others involved may potentially be at risk for health consequences such as cancer due to contact with toxins. A washdown after an emergency is crucial in reducing the amount of harmful chemicals when leaving the scene.

After a Stroke: Ongoing Brain Effects

After a stroke, one must be wary of some effects of the stroke. According to Johns Hopkins Medicine, the brain may experience a multitude of problems in the foreseeable future. Strokes can cause the loss of bodily control, impaired ability to read, write, learn information, and inhibited memory. It is crucial to monitor rehabilitation to properly mitigate the risk of a subsequent stroke.
