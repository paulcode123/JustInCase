The Problem with Emergency Services

immediate. Knowing the causes of delays and ways to be prepared may save a life. Read Time: 3-4 mins When a person decides to make a 911 call, he or she anticipates the delivery of some assistance within a few minutes. Nonetheless, the response times of emergencies are on the rise in the majority of regions in the United States. The National Fire Protection

Association reports that the response time has been increasing over the past decade due to the increase in the number of calls and the resource shortage. The slightest time wastage may be life-threatening; in extreme situations, each minute without attention in cases (like cardiac arrest) may mean the difference between life and death. Due to this, it is valuable to learn the reasons behind emergency services failing, and what individuals can do to get ready to enhance their personal safety and overall community awareness.

Increasing pressure on emergency services is one of the greatest causes of slowness in responding. The number of citizens that relied on 911 services is increasing more than ever before, along with the expansion of population and community development. Nevertheless, the quantity of ambulances, firefighters, and dispatchers does not always grow at the same pace. This leaves a disparity between demand and resources. According to the National Fire

Protection Association, the majority of departments in the country are getting more and more calls with fewer resources in terms of staffing and funding. This imbalance would lead to more wait times during the peak hours or huge emergencies. For example, in the case of a natural disaster or a large-scale accident, emergency teams will be overwhelmed, resulting in some of the calls being postponed or solved with limited resources. Even in the case of normal situations, factors like traffic, roads, and proximity to emergency stations can add minutes of time that are vitally important.

The other critical problem is the fact that people are not ready. General beliefs are that emergency services will be there in time and will be able to take control of the situation, but the first few minutes of an emergency are usually the most important. However, in the case of the lack of early intervention, the situation of an individual can become worse until the delivery of treatment. Basic abilities such as CPR, AED, or knowledge of how to act in case of a fire will be of great help. The American Red Cross statistics demonstrate that two or even three times more lives may be saved in case of cardiac arrest due to immediate bystander intervention. However, many individuals are not trained or are not confident enough to intervene in case of an emergency. In addition to this, a lack of planning can make the situation increasingly complicated. For example, families that do not have an escape strategy may be unsure of what to do in a dangerous situation, leading to less time for primary responders to intervene.

Emergency services are highly crucial to the safety of the community, and they are not always urgent. The demand and lack of resources, combined with an overall lack of readiness, can end in catastrophe. Individuals can contribute remarkably through their awareness of such problems and through easy actions, such as knowing CPR, practicing emergency procedures, and being in a position to identify dangers.
