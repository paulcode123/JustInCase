Stopping Severe Bleeding

During an emergency, immediate action can save lives. Read this article to learn how to efficiently treat severe bleeding to avoid blood loss.

Bleeding is one of the top causes of preventable death following trauma. A human being can lose a considerable amount of blood in a matter of minutes in the case of an emergency, such as a car accident, fall, or any accident. Heavy bleeding control is a life-saving skill that can be done until EMS arrives.

Check the Scene and Apply Direct Pressure

When caring for severe bleeding, the first and most critical step is to ensure that the area is safe. Emergency services should be contacted immediately (or another person should contact 911). The American Red Cross (American Red Cross, 2024) suggests applying direct pressure to the wound with a clean cloth, bandage, or cloth if no medical supplies are available. Press the area and wrap it up to prevent further bleeding. If blood soaks the dressing, then place additional dressing layers over the initial layer.

When to Use a Tourniquet

Sometimes direct pressure is not enough to prevent blood from spilling, and you may need a tourniquet for wounds on an arm or leg. If direct pressure is not enough to stop a life-threatening extremity bleeding, the American College of Surgeons (American College of Surgeons, 2023) suggests that a tourniquet should be applied to stop a life-threatening extremity bleeding. The tourniquet should be placed several inches above the wound to prevent blood from flowing into it and to stop the bleeding. It is to be removed only by medical personnel as per the application.

Watch for Signs of Shock

First, the bleeding needs to be stopped, and for the injured person, any signs of shock need to be treated immediately. Mayo Clinic (2024) describes symptoms of shock as pale or cool skin, fast breathing, weakness, confusion, dizziness, or unconsciousness. If suspected to be in shock, it is best to get the injured person on his back and keep him warm until first aid/medical assistance arrives.

A Skill Worth Learning

It is an important skill to learn how to stop excessive bleeding in an emergency. If prompt measures are taken, direct pressure or a tourniquet, and the knowledge of shock can stabilize an injured person until professional medical care can be provided. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2024) estimate that there won't be an advanced medical treatment available until somebody has been immediately treated for bleeding.
