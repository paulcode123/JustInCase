What to Do in the First 5 Minutes of an Emergency

Ensure the area is actually safe before you run in and do anything. According to the American Red Cross, assessing the scene helps you provide better care for the victim and stops you from becoming another victim. For example, if your friend fell down the stairs, you should first look around for possible problems like broken objects or a crowded space. Then, check the person. Are they conscious? Breathing? Do they respond when you say their name? This tells you how serious the situation is and what you should do next. After checking, you need to call for help. Calling 911 as soon as possible ensures that emergency responders are on the way, and it’s especially important to do so if the person is unresponsive or in a life-threatening emergency. When calling, stay calm and explain where you are, what happened, and the person’s condition. The Mid-Atlantic Emergency Medical Associates notes that catching warning signs early on is what determines the kind of care someone actually needs, so provide as many details as you can. Dispatchers are actually trained to help you through what to do while you wait. So put the phone on speaker and listen to them. And even if you’re not sure if the situation is actually serious enough for a 911 call, it’s always better to call than regret it and potentially cause the person to be in a worse condition. While you wait for help, care for the person. You can keep them still after a fall, applying pressure to a wound, or helping them stay conscious. These simple actions in the first five minutes can actually help stop the situation from getting worse while waiting for professionals.
