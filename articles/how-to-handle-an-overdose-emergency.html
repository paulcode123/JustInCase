How to Handle an Overdose Emergency

During an overdose emergency it's highly important to know how to handle the situation properly. However, what if you are unsure and you don't know what to do? From reading this article, you will be able to understand the definition of an overdose, the symptoms, how to handle it, and how to respond properly–which is crucial in saving a life.

According to the National Center for Drug Abuse Statistics, over 105,000 people die from drug overdoses in a year. Just imagine a situation: your friend, family member, or even a stranger, suddenly becomes unresponsive after taking a substance. They're no longer breathing, their skin is turning blue, and now fear is starting to arise in you. However, what exactly is an overdose, and what are the symptoms? Being prepared for a situation like this means recognizing the symptoms and knowing how to respond is vital to be able to create the best possibility to save a life.

What Is an Overdose?

As stated by the Department of Health, "Overdose happens when your body is overwhelmed with a toxic amount of a substance or combination of substances." When an overdose occurs, it is an extremely important step in handling this type of emergency to be able to recognize the symptoms. It can be very hard to differentiate whether someone is truly overdosing or simply intoxicated or high. Thus, it is important to recognize the following symptoms.

Recognizing the Symptoms

Opioid Overdose Warning Signs

  • Loss of consciousness: The person is unresponsive and cannot be woken.
  • Irregular breathing: Breathing may be slow, shallow, or stop entirely; heart rate may be irregular.
  • Speech impairment: Slurred speech or inability to speak coherently.
  • Skin color changes: Lips, skin, or fingernails may turn blue, gray, or pale.
  • Choking sounds: A snore-like gurgling noise may indicate airway obstruction.
  • Vomiting: The person may vomit while unconscious, creating an aspiration risk.
  • Limpness: The body may go limp with little to no muscle tone.

If you're still having a hard time determining whether someone is overdosing, it is best to stay on the side of caution, and to treat the situation as if it is an overdose.

Stimulate, Call 911, and Position the Person

The most important step to handling this emergency is to act right away and stay calm. First, stimulate the person. Either try to wake them up through a physical or verbal stimulation. This can look like: pinching them, rubbing your knuckles on their sternum, or telling them you're going to call first responders. If the person does not respond to anything, do not continue stimulating them. Immediately call 911. Try to provide as much relevant information in the call as possible, such as location, responsiveness, and potential drug or opioid. Place the person in a recovery position, laying them on their side, bending their knee, and turning their face to the side.

Rescue Breathing

Rescue breathing is essential to this process. To administer rescue breathing:

  1. Place the person on their back and tilt their chin up.
  2. Check if there is anything blocking the airway, and if so, remove it.
  3. Plug their nose with one hand, give 2 breaths, and blow enough air to make their chest rise.
  4. If the chest does not rise, tilt their head back more and try again.
  5. Continue administering one breath every 5 seconds.

Administering Naloxone

If, and only if, you are able to recognize symptoms of an opioid overdose, prepare to administer naloxone to the person.

Nasal Spray

According to the Natural Harm Reduction Coalition, these are the proper steps for nasal spray naloxone:

  1. Do rescue breathing for a few quick breaths if the person is not breathing.
  2. Pry off the yellow cap, then the red cap.
  3. Grip the clear plastic wings, gently screwing the capsule of naloxone into the barrel of the syringe.
  4. Insert the white cone into the nostril, giving a short vigorous push on the end of the capsule to spray the naloxone into the nose.
  5. One half of the capsule should go into each nostril. If there is no reaction in 2-5 minutes, give a second dose.

Injectable Form

If the naloxone comes in an injectable form, the Natural Harm Reduction Coalition states to follow these steps:

  1. Check the expiration date, then do rescue breathing for a few quick breaths if the person is not breathing.
  2. Pop off the orange top vial, and draw up 1cc/1mL/100u of naloxone.
  3. Inject it into a muscle. The thigh, upper/outer quadrant of the butt, and shoulder are very good options for injection.
  4. Inject it straight in to make sure to hit the muscle, and continue rescue breathing for 2-3 minutes.

In both cases, if the patient still is not responding after receiving naloxone and rescue breathing, immediately begin cardiopulmonary resuscitation from a trained bystander and the emergency medical system. If there is no naloxone kit nearby, continue rescue breathing until help arrives.

Stay Until Help Arrives

To make sure the person stays safe, stay until emergency services arrive, and try to keep them awake. Opioid overdoses are one of the most lethal accidents in this world. However, with your help and knowledge, saving a life can be more than possible. It's extremely important to stay calm, and to keep the symptoms and proper procedure in mind when handling an opioid overdose, and encourage friends and family members to obtain this knowledge as well. With everybody's help, opioids can take a lot less lives.
