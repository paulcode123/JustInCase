All of these are classified as medical emergencies and require immediate attention by a medical professional, but you should still seek help if you’re unsure. How do you seek help? First, ensure that you are safe or in a safe place. Take a deep breath. Then, call 911 or ask someone to call emergency services for you. Stay calm and provide the operator with the exact details– your location, what the medical emergency is, and what happened– and follow the operator’s instructions. Don’t hang up the call unless they tell you to. You can also drive the person experiencing the medical emergency to the nearest hospital, unless you are experiencing the emergency yourself. However, it’s generally advised against because medical responders can begin treatment immediately upon arrival, which is often faster than driving yourself. Finally, after calling 911 or while 911 is being called, perform CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation) if necessary. Do not attempt this if it’s unsafe to be in close proximity to the person experiencing the medical emergency or if you don’t know the proper technique. You can also place the person experiencing the emergency in the recovery position if they are unconscious and it is safe. According to the American Red Cross, you should place them on their left side, with one arm up so they can lay their head on that arm. This helps keep the airways clear of blood, vomit, or any other fluid in the airway. Take the other hand and place it against their face, as if they’re sleeping on a pillow. Bend one or both of their knees up to stabilize them. This allows you to monitor their airway and any changes that may occur.
